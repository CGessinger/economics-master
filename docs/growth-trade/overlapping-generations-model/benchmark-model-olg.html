The Benchmark OLG Model

Economic Growth II (M.Sc. UB)
The Diamond OLG model with two-period-lived households, where the savings of the young build the capital stock of the next period.

The basic overlapping generations model with capital accumulation was introduced by Diamond (1965), however this summary is mostly based on De la Croix and Michel (2002).

In the benchmark Diamond economy each household lives for two periods. When young, it works, earns a wage, consumes part of this income and saves the rest. When old, it is retired and consumes the gross return on the savings it made when young. Through these individual saving decisions, the young generation builds the capital stock that firms use in the next period.

The Benchmark Model

  • Time is discrete: \(t = 0, 1, 2, 3, \dots\)
  • Each individual lives for two periods. At every date \(t\) two generations coexist:
    • Young (born at \(t\)), size \(N_t\)
    • Old (born at \(t-1\)), size \(N_{t-1}\)
  • Population grows at the constant rate \(n\):

\[ n = \frac{N_t}{N_{t-1}} - 1 \iff \frac{N_t}{N_{t-1}} = 1+n \]

  • Only the young work. Each young individual supplies one unit of labor inelastically, so labor equals the size of the young generation:

\[ L_t = N_t \]

  • The young earn the wage \(w_t\), consume \(c_t\) and save \(s_t\). When old, they consume \(d_{t+1}\) out of their savings.

Household Problem

Preferences

The lifetime utility of an individual born at \(t\) depends on consumption when young and when old:

\[ U_t = u(c_t, d_{t+1}) \]

Budget Constraints

\[ \text{Young:}\quad c_t + s_t = w_t \]

\[ \text{Old:}\quad d_{t+1} = R_{t+1}\, s_t \;\Rightarrow\; s_t = \frac{d_{t+1}}{R_{t+1}} \]

\(R_{t+1}\) is the gross return on savings. Substituting \(s_t\) gives the lifetime budget constraint:

\[ c_t + \frac{d_{t+1}}{R_{t+1}} = w_t \]

Optimization

The household takes \(w_t\) and \(R_{t+1}\) as given and solves

\[ \max_{c_t,\, d_{t+1} \ge 0} \; u(c_t, d_{t+1}) \quad \text{s.t.} \quad w_t = c_t + \frac{d_{t+1}}{R_{t+1}} \]

\[ \mathcal{L} = u(c_t, d_{t+1}) + \lambda_t \left( w_t - c_t - \frac{d_{t+1}}{R_{t+1}} \right) \]

F.O.C.:

\[ \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial c_t} = u_{c_t} - \lambda_t = 0 \qquad (1) \]

\[ \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial d_{t+1}} = u_{d_{t+1}} - \frac{\lambda_t}{R_{t+1}} = 0 \qquad (2) \]

\[ \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \lambda_t} = w_t - c_t - \frac{d_{t+1}}{R_{t+1}} = 0 \qquad (3) \]

From (1), \(\lambda_t = u_{c_t}\). Replacing in (2):

\[ u_{d_{t+1}} = \frac{u_{c_t}}{R_{t+1}} \]

The marginal utility of one unit consumed when old equals the marginal utility of the \(1/R_{t+1}\) units given up when young.

Firms Problem

Technology

Firms produce output with capital \(K_t\) and labor \(L_t\) using a production function \(F(K_t, L_t)\) with constant returns to scale. For \(\lambda > 0\):

\[ F(\lambda K_t, \lambda L_t) = \lambda F(K_t, L_t) \]

Setting \(\lambda = \frac{1}{L_t}\) gives the intensive form with capital per worker \(k_t = \frac{K_t}{L_t}\):

\[ f(k_t) = F(k_t, 1) = \frac{F(K_t, L_t)}{L_t} \iff F(K_t, L_t) = L_t f(k_t) \]

The marginal products then only depend on \(k_t\):

\[ \frac{\partial F(K_t, L_t)}{\partial K_t} = f'(k_t), \qquad \frac{\partial F(K_t, L_t)}{\partial L_t} = f(k_t) - k_t f'(k_t) \]

Optimization

Firms rent capital at the rental rate \(q_t\) and hire labor at the wage \(w_t\). They take both prices as given and maximize profits:

\[ \max_{K_t, L_t} \; \pi_t = F(K_t, L_t) - w_t L_t - q_t K_t \]

F.O.C.:

\[ \frac{\partial \pi_t}{\partial K_t} = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; f'(k_t) = q_t \]

\[ \frac{\partial \pi_t}{\partial L_t} = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; f(k_t) - k_t f'(k_t) = w_t \]

Each factor is paid its marginal product. Capital depreciates at rate \(\delta\), so the gross return on savings is the rental rate plus the undepreciated capital:

\[ R_t = 1 + r_t = q_t + 1 - \delta \;\Rightarrow\; q_t = r_t + \delta \]
